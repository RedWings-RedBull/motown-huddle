## Where the division stands

After six weeks, Minnesota is the only unbeaten club in the NFC North. The Vikings are 5-0 (2-0 in the division) on a five-game winning streak, with 109 points scored, 64 allowed and a +45 differential, the best margin in the group by a wide distance.

Green Bay and Chicago are both 4-2, and the standings list head-to-head as the tiebreaker between them. The Packers have won three straight and are 1-1 in the division. Their 127-125 scoring split leaves them at +2, the thinnest margin of the four, so the record is running well ahead of the scoreboard. The Bears are 0-2 in division play but have the better margin, 150 points for and 119 against for a +31. They have won one straight.

Detroit is 3-2 and has yet to play a division game, so its 0-0 divisional record is a blank rather than a problem. The Lions have scored 164 points, the most in the division, but have allowed 148 and sit at +16. They also have a one-game winning streak.

## What the simulations say

Across 10,000 simulations, Minnesota wins the division 75% of the time and makes the playoffs in 98.3%, with 12.6 expected wins. Nobody else is close on the division title.

The chasers are bunched:

- **Chicago:** 10.8% to win the division, 78% to make the playoffs, 10.3 expected wins.
- **Detroit:** 9.3% division, 62.7% playoffs, 9.6 expected wins.
- **Green Bay:** 4.9% division, 57.8% playoffs, 9.3 expected wins.

The Packers are 4-2 but have the lowest division and playoff odds of the three. The 3-2 Lions project ahead of Green Bay in expected wins, so the simulation is not leaning on the win-loss record alone. Chicago has the best odds of the three chasers on both measures, which stands out for a club that has not yet won a division game.

## Does efficiency back up the records?

**Minnesota:** Mostly yes, with a caveat about where the wins come from. The Vikings' defense leads the league at -0.210 EPA per play, rank 1. The offense is the weak side at -0.099, rank 27. The defense has carried the offense so far, and the +45 margin shows it. The 12.6 expected wins reflect that, but the ceiling depends on the offense not sliding further.

**Detroit:** This is the split profile. The offense is third at 0.180 EPA per play. The defense is 31st at 0.152, where a positive number is bad for a defense. The 164 points scored fit the offensive rank, and the 148 allowed fit the defensive one. The record is fine, and the margin is modest because the two halves pull in opposite directions.

**Chicago:** The efficiency is average, not elite. The offense is 13th at 0.025 EPA per play and the defense is 15th at -0.032. A +31 differential is a bit more than those metrics would suggest, yet the 78% playoff figure is second-best in the division, so the model credits the Bears for their results.

**Green Bay:** The defense is the strength, seventh at -0.056 EPA per play. The offense is 15th at -0.010. That decent but unspectacular profile fits a +2 differential better than it fits 4-2. The three-game winning streak has outrun the underlying numbers.
